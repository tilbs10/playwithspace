## The Setup

A good, close game. We won 35-30.

The other team had one player who was far taller than anyone on our side, and she changed what was available to us. Shots and layups we'd normally get were often taken away.

## What I Saw

**The height put pressure on our shot selection.** A layup that's usually there isn't there when a much taller player is waiting at the rim. The girls had to find something else.

**The drive, draw and dish became our best option.** Nobody drew this up. Over the course of the game it happened naturally: drive, pull the defence in, and pass it off to the teammate who'd been left open.

**Defensive rebounds mattered.** With a tall player on their side, limiting their second chances became critical. Every rebound we secured was one less shot for her.

**And once we had the ball, we protected it.** Ball high or at the chin, then the pivot to find the outlet pass. That's the exact problem from [Game 6](/blog/game-6-bring-it-down), where the catch from the rebound and rushed outlets were the concern.

## The Moment

No single play — the moment was the whole team. All the girls contributed, and the improvement showed up the way it has been lately: through the course of the game, not just from one week to the next.

## What It Teaches

**When the shot is taken away, the pass is still there.** A drive doesn't have to end in a shot to be a good drive. If it pulls a defender in — especially the tall one — someone else is open. That's the same idea as [Playing Off the Gravity](/blog/playing-off-the-gravity), just on a smaller scale: the drive bends the defence, and the dish uses what it bent.

**The girls found it themselves.** The draw and dish wasn't an instruction from the bench. They read what the game was giving them and adjusted to it. That's the central question of this season in one habit: can they see the game?

**The rebound work carried over.** [Session 2](/blog/session-2-protect-the-ball) introduced the pivot and ear to back pocket. Game 6 showed the rebound catch was still the weak link. Here, against a taller team, the girls secured the ball high, pivoted and looked for the outlet. That's the work showing up when it counted.
