## The Setup

We knew what was coming. This team plays aggressive, right in the girls' faces, so that's what I talked about before the game.

The answer was the pivot from [Session 2](/blog/session-2-protect-the-ball): where you place the ball, and where you place your body. Put your body between the defender and the ball, and the pressure has nothing to reach for.

We won 53-6. It was a good game, but it didn't start like one.

## What I Saw

**The first quarter was pretty even.** We couldn't really get going. The pressure early on was high, and it took the girls time to settle into it.

**Then they got comfortable.** Once they did, we got cleaner rebounds, started to create a few fast break opportunities, and the girls relaxed into the game. They played a lot better from there.

**The catch from the rebound was the main concern.** The girls are getting to the ball. What happens next is the problem: bringing it down safely after they've rebounded it.

**A lot of our outlet passes were rushed.** They know the outlet exists now, which is progress. But the pass was leaving before anyone had looked.

## The Moment

The change after the first quarter. Nothing about the other team changed; the pressure was the same. What changed was how the girls felt about it. Once being crowded stopped being a surprise, they could play.

That's the pivot doing its job. It doesn't remove the pressure. It gives a player a way to stay calm inside it.

## What It Teaches

**Comfort under pressure is a skill, and it showed up mid-game.** In [Game 1](/blog/game-1-trial-by-fire) the physicality was new and it rattled them. Here it rattled them for a quarter, and then they adjusted. That's the same growth, arriving faster.

**A rebound isn't finished when you catch it.** Securing it, protecting it and finding the outlet are three separate jobs. We introduced the outlet box in Session 2 and built it into the drills in [Session 3](/blog/session-3-keep-moving). The girls now pass to it; the next step is passing to it *well*.

**A rushed outlet is a wasted break.** The outlet is the first pass of our [transition](/blog/passing-ahead-in-transition). If it's thrown before the rebounder has seen the floor, the fast break is over before it starts.

## Next Session Focus

**The rebound, in order:** take the rebound, pivot, scan the floor, then look for a good outlet pass. Slowing the first second down is what makes the rest fast.

**The screen away.** We'll keep working on it from [Session 4](/blog/session-4-the-pass-that-helps), in preparation for the next game.
