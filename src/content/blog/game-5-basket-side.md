## The Setup

A comfortable win, 53-6. We'd beaten this same team 52-6 last time, so the girls did well again.

As I said after [Game 2](/blog/game-2-hands-up-eyes-open), a win like this isn't proof of anything. The other team was weaker, and I'd rather use the night for what it's good for.

## What I Saw

**They're getting back on defence.** That's the thing I keep coming back to. [Game 3](/blog/game-3-the-space-behind-you) was about the girls beginning to understand *why* we get back, and tonight it looked like a habit rather than an instruction.

**Their positioning wasn't tested.** This is the honest part. A lot of what I want from our defence — where we stand relative to the basket and the ball — never got exposed, because the other team couldn't exploit it. When a defender steps up and leaves the floor open behind her, a good attacker punishes it. Tonight nobody did.

That means the habit I'm worried about is still there, just hidden by the scoreboard.

## The Moment

No single play. What stood out was confidence.

The drills from [last training](/blog/session-3-keep-moving) clearly landed. The girls moved like they knew what they were doing — passing on the move, keeping the ball going — because they'd just done it a hundred times in a row. Confidence comes from reps, and you could see it.

## What It Teaches

**Being basket side is the answer to the space behind you.** In Game 3 I wrote that every step toward the ball is a step away from the space behind you. The fix is to stay *basket side* — between the player you're guarding and the basket — at all times. Then if the attacker beats you, the basket is still behind you, not an open lane.

That's the thing I'll talk about at training. Not as a correction, but as a question: *where do you want to be when she goes past you?* And the risk of getting it wrong: every time a player gets behind us, we're giving up a clear run at the rim.

**Weekly drills are how the confidence stays.** The drills from last session gave the girls confidence, so they're not a one-off. We'll run them every week. Skills the girls can do without thinking are the ones that hold when the game gets harder — and it will, eventually, against a team that can pass.

## Next Session Focus

**Defence: basket side, always.** Where to stand, why it matters, and what it costs us when someone gets behind.

**The weekly drills** — the same ones, as a regular part of every session.
