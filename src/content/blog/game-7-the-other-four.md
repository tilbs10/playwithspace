## The Setup

The top team. The only team we'd lost to this season — [twice](/blog/game-4-denied).

It was a tough game. It was poorly officiated, which put a lot of pressure on the girls, and the opposition, who were used to winning easily, stepped up how aggressively they played as soon as the score stayed close.

We won 12-10. For a team that lost 38-6 to this group in [our very first game](/blog/game-1-trial-by-fire), I'll let that number sit for a second.

## What I Saw

**Our best player had the most attention all game.** Same plan as [Game 4](/blog/game-4-denied): deny her the ball. This time it didn't decide the game, because the other girls got to the right spots on the floor.

**The screen away worked.** We'd only worked on it at training once, in [Session 4](/blog/session-4-the-pass-that-helps). We used it, and it was effective.

**The girls managed the pressure.** Rough calls, a team getting more physical as the game tightened — and they held on.

## The Moment

The second quarter. We fell behind, and I called a time out.

What came back on the floor was better spacing. The girls spread out, got to their spots, and we came back to take the lead into half time.

That's the thing I watched them do in Game 2 — take an instruction from a break and put it into the game — but this time against the best team in the league, behind on the scoreboard, with the pressure at its highest.

## What It Teaches

**This is Game 4, answered.** I wrote then that a team that depends on one player getting the ball has a gravity problem, because the defence only has to solve one player. Against the same team, with the same plan aimed at our best player, the other girls getting to the right spots meant denying her actually cost them something. It's the whole idea of [Great Players Don't Create Space Alone](/blog/great-players-dont-create-space-alone), in a 12-10 game.

**The screen away is a spacing tool.** It gives the player who has just passed a job, and it moves a defender — which matters most when the defence is fixed on one player.

**The spacing came from a time out, not a play.** We didn't draw anything new up. We reminded them where the space was, and they went and found it.

## Next Session Focus

**Defensive shape.** Understanding the parts of the court we want to protect, so we take driving lanes away instead of giving them up.

**A press break.** We'll put one in and practise it before we play this team again.

**Full-court spacing and timing**, and the types of passes it takes to get the ball down the court. There are only four teams in the league, so we'll have a few games to get it right.
