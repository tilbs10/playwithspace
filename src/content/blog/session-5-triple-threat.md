## The Setup

Only three girls at training. That ruled out the team work I'd planned — you can't run a press break with three — so we went back to the individual skills that everything else is built on.

## What We Worked On

**Shooting form and footwork.** Most of the session. The basics of a good shot: how the feet are set, and how the body is lined up before the ball goes up.

**The triple threat on the catch.** Every catch finished in the triple threat position: ball protected, knees bent, facing the basket — ready to shoot, pass or drive. Catching in triple threat means the shot isn't something you scramble into. It's already there.

**One-on-one defence: force her to the sideline or baseline.** We finished with defensive principles, one on one. The defender's job is to push the ball-handler towards the sideline or the baseline, and away from the middle of the floor.

## What It Teaches

**The catch decides the shot.** In [Session 4](/blog/session-4-the-pass-that-helps) we worked on getting to the spot first and passing to help the scorer. The triple threat is the receiver's half of that deal: arrive, catch ready, and the good pass turns into a good shot.

**Triple threat is a threat to the defence, not just a stance.** A player who can shoot, pass or drive from the catch makes her defender guess. That's useful against pressure like we saw in [Game 7](/blog/game-7-the-other-five): a defender who has to respect three options can't commit to one.

**The lines are an extra defender.** Forcing the ball-handler to the sideline or baseline takes half the floor away from her. It's how we start [controlling the dribble](/blog/game-3-the-space-behind-you) instead of chasing it, and it's the first piece of the defensive shape we said we needed after Game 7: if the drive goes along the line, the driving lanes through the middle stay shut.

**A small group isn't a lost session.** Three players means more touches each. The team concepts can wait a week; the footwork and the stance are what make the team concepts work.
