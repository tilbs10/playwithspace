## Part 1: Themes That Keep Coming Back

Five ideas show up again and again across the season, usually without being
named. Each one could carry a deep-dive or a short series.

### Theme A: Time Is the Hidden Currency
**Shows up in:** G2 (ball security bought time for spacing), S2 ("protecting
the ball buys time, and time buys space"), S3 (quick decisions), G6 (rushed
outlets; "slowing the first second down is what makes the rest fast"), S4
(get to the spot *before* the ball).

**The tension worth writing about:** we asked for *quick decisions* in S3 and
*slow down the first second* in G6. Both are right. The skill is knowing
which second to slow down and which to speed up. Calm at the catch, fast
after it.

**No evergreen post covers this yet.** *Rhythm* and *The Run Starts Before the
Pass* are close, but neither treats time as something you can buy, spend or
waste.

### Theme B: Defend the Space Behind You, Not the Ball in Front of You
**Shows up in:** G1 (everyone chased the ball), G2 (hands up, read the next
pass), G3 (stepping up opens the floor behind you), G5 (basket side),
S5 (the sideline and baseline as extra defenders), G7 (defensive shape,
taking driving lanes away).

**The season arc is the story:** chase the ball → get back → beat them to the
spot → stay basket side → use the lines → protect lanes as a unit. That's a
complete progression for teaching youth defence, built in the order it
actually happened.

**No evergreen defence post exists** apart from the foul post. This is the
biggest gap on the site, which is all about offensive spacing so far.

### Theme C: The Other Four
**Shows up in:** G4 (they denied our best player and we had no answer), G7
(same plan, but the other girls got to their spots and we won 12-10), S4
(the screen away gives the passer a job), S5 (triple threat makes a
defender guess).

**The story:** G4 → G7 is the cleanest before-and-after of the season.
*Great Players Don't Create Space Alone* and *Playing Off the Gravity* cover
the theory. This would be the practical version: what a coach actually does
when the opposition takes away the best player.

### Theme D: Coaching in One Sentence
**Shows up in:** G2 (one cue per break, then watch), G1 (they improved inside
a single game with no coaching), G6 (adjusted within a quarter), G7 (the
time-out produced spacing, not a play), philosophy ("one concept at a time,
embodied before moving on").

**The story:** the most consistent thing this season isn't a basketball
skill. It's how fast these girls learn from *one* clear instruction plus a
chance to try it. It's a method post, and it's useful to any coach of any
sport.

### Theme E: A Spot Is Only Useful If You Can Use It
**Shows up in:** G4 ("standing in the right spot is one skill; being
comfortable there is another"), S5 (triple threat on the catch), S4 (the
pass that helps the receiver score), G2 (pressure handling).

**The story:** the site argues hard for spacing. This is the honest
counterweight: spacing without the skills to act from the spot is just
standing far apart. It sharpens the site's core argument instead of
weakening it.

## Part 3: Going Deeper on Three Themes

### Deep Dive 1: Defend the Space (Theme B)
*Could be one long post, or a four-part series to match the World Cup series.*

**Thesis:** Kids defend the ball because that's where the action looks like
it is. Good defence starts by defending the basket and the space behind you,
and letting the attack come to you.

**Possible structure:**
1. *The instinct.* G1: everyone went to the ball. It produced steals and gave
   up layups. Why chasing feels like defending.
2. *The space behind you.* G3: every step towards the ball is a step away from
   the basket. Diagram: the defender steps up and the lane opens behind her.
3. *Basket side.* G5: "Where do you want to be when she goes past you?" Teach
   it as a question, not a correction.
4. *Using the lines.* S5: force to the sideline or baseline and half the floor
   disappears.
5. *Defending as five.* G7 → the next step: protecting the lanes as a unit
   (help side, first rotation, which is "The One Thing" in the philosophy).

**Open questions for you:**
- Is the half-court press the end point of this series, or its own post?
- Do you have a drill for basket side yet? A post lands better with one.
- Diagrams: the court components already exist, so a "space behind you"
  diagram is cheap to make.

### Deep Dive 2: Time Is the Hidden Currency (Theme A)
**Thesis:** Every skill we've taught this season either buys time (pivot,
ball security, triple threat), spends it well (get to the spot first, scan
before the outlet) or takes it away from the defence (quick decisions,
passing ahead). Coaching kids is mostly teaching them where the time is.

**Possible structure:**
1. *Pressure is a shortage of time.* G1 and G6: physicality rattled them
   because it removed time.
2. *Buying time:* the pivot, ear to back pocket, triple threat.
3. *Giving time to teammates:* G2. When the ball-handler is under control,
   everyone else can arrive at their spots instead of rushing.
4. *Slow the first second:* the G6 rebound sequence.
5. *Taking time from the defence:* S3 quick decisions, passing ahead
   ("the ball moves faster than any defender can run", from the S6 plan).

**Why it's strong:** it ties the site's existing threads (spacing, rhythm,
transition) into one frame. It could become the site's signature idea next to
"the five kinds of space": *space and time*.

### Deep Dive 3: The Other Four (Theme C)
**Thesis:** A team with one star has a gravity problem in the wrong direction.
The fix isn't to force the ball to her. It's to make every other player a
reason the denial costs something.

**Possible structure:**
1. *Game 4:* the pattern. The ball goes towards her, doesn't arrive, and
   nobody else can turn it into anything.
2. *Diagnosis:* two separate problems. Spacing (wrong spots) and comfort
   (wrong skills in those spots). This is where Theme E comes in.
3. *What we built:* quick decisions (S3), the screen away (S4), triple
   threat (S5).
4. *Game 7:* same plan from the opposition, a different answer from us.
   12-10.
5. *For coaches:* a checklist for when your best player is denied.

**Open question:** there's a development angle here too. The non-stars
getting better is the real win, and that's a message parents care about.

## Part 4: Content Strategy Notes

- **Defence is the gap.** The site has 11 evergreen posts and almost all are
  offence and spacing. The season posts are full of defence. Theme B fills
  that gap with evidence you already have.
- **Series format works.** The World Cup series already exists. A "Defend the
  Space" series would match it and give the newsletter a run of related posts.
- **Season recap / lead magnet.** Themes A–E together are close to a
  "What one U12 season taught me" guide, which could be a newsletter
  sign-up incentive at the end of the season.
- **Netball crossover (#6)** is the one topic with a different audience.
  It's worth doing for reach alone.
