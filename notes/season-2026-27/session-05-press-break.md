# Session 5 — Beating the Press: Pass It Down the Floor

**Theme:** Against full-court man-to-man pressure, use a screen away to get the
ball in, pass it down the floor instead of dribbling it, and turn it into two
phases of a fast break
**Date:** TBC (before the next game against the top team)
**Players:** Squad — built for 5; notes below for 4 or 6
**Duration:** 60 mins
**Equipment:** Full court, a ball per pair, cones (6–8) to mark the starting
spots, bibs for the defence
**Why this session:** Game 7 (W 12-10) against the top team: an aggressive,
in-your-face side that lifted its pressure as soon as the score was close, with
our best player under the most attention all game. They'll pressure us full
court next time. Because they play man-to-man, this isn't a traditional press
break: every player is guarded, so we get open with a screen and we move the
ball with passes. Three goals:
1. **Save the PG's legs** — the ball travels down the floor; she doesn't carry it.
2. **Layups** — look for two phases of a fast break every time.
3. **Make pressing cost them** — if their press gives up layups, they'll drop
   back into a half-court defence, which is where we've built our spacing all
   season.

---

## The Question to Hold All Session

*"Who's ahead of the ball?"*

The ball moves faster than any defender can run. Every pass ahead puts a
defender behind the play — and a defender behind the play isn't guarding
anyone.

---

## The Shape — Baseline Inbound

Five starting spots. Walk it before you run it.

```
            (the basket we're attacking)
   ------------------------------------------------
   |                                              |
   |                    L                         |   L   LONG: at the free-throw line
   |                                              |       at the attacking end.
   |                                              |       Reads Phase 1.
   |----------------------C-----------------------|   C   CENTRE: in the centre circle.
   |                                              |       The pass ahead.
   |                                              |
   |       PG  <----- S                           |   PG, S  start on opposite wings in
   |                    (S crosses to screen)     |       the back court. S screens away
   |                                              |       for the PG.
   ------------------I-----------------------------   I   INBOUNDER: on the baseline.
            (the basket we defend)                        Becomes the trailer — Phase 2.
```

**The sequence:**

1. **Screen to get it in.** S crosses and screens the PG's defender. The PG sets
   her defender up, then comes off the screen, shoulder to shoulder, back to the
   ball. If the defence switches or both defenders go with the PG, S opens back
   to the ball — she's the second option.
2. **Pass it ahead.** Once the ball is in, the first look is up the floor. C
   flashes towards the ball to receive the pass ahead. The PG doesn't have to
   dribble the length of the court — two or three passes get us there.
3. **Phase 1 — is it on?** If there's a clear lane, or more of us than them
   ahead of the ball, go now: L cuts to the rim and the ball goes in for the
   layup.
4. **Phase 2 — the trailer.** If Phase 1 isn't on, the ball goes to the top and
   the inbounder arrives last as the trailer: a strong side cut or a lap
   post-up, the same Phase 2 we use on every fast break.
5. **If she's doubled:** pivot and protect (ear to back pocket). Two defenders
   on one player means a teammate is free. Find her.

---

## Warm-up — 8 mins

**The weekly drills: three-girl weave, full court**
- Two trips each, then add the rule: no dribbling at all
- Coaching cue: *"Pass ahead of her, so she runs onto it."*
- This is the whole session in miniature — the ball beats the defence down
  the floor

---

## Block 1 — 10 mins
*Instruction (2 mins) + Drill (8 mins)*

**Concept:** The screen away gets the ball in

**Drill: 2v2 plus the inbounder**
- PG and S on opposite back-court wings, two defenders face-guarding them,
  inbounder on the baseline
- Coach counts five seconds out loud — that's the rule they'll face in a game
- S crosses and screens; the PG waits for it, then comes off it to the ball
- Second round: the defence switches the screen. S opens back to the ball and
  the inbounder finds her
- Rotate every 3 reps so everyone plays PG, screener and inbounder
- Coaching cue (PG): *"Wait for the screen. Then go — shoulder to shoulder."*
- Coaching cue (S): *"After you screen, show your hands. You might be the open one."*
- Coaching cue (inbounder): *"Look at both. You have five seconds — use three."*

---

## Block 2 — 10 mins
*Instruction (2 mins) + Drill (8 mins)*

**Concept:** The right pass to beat pressure

Three passes, one job each. Show each once, then go.
- **Overhead pass:** over a hands-up defender, or long to L
- **Bounce pass:** past the defender's hands when they're up high
- **Chest pass:** on the move, to a teammate with nobody between

**Drill: The pass-ahead chain (inbound → PG → C → L)**
- No defence first: inbound to the PG, PG catches and pivots to face the floor,
  pass ahead to C flashing, C to L cutting to the rim for the layup
- Then add one defender on the PG and one on C — hands up, no reaching
- One rule: the ball must be passed, not dribbled, until it reaches L
- Coaching cue: *"Catch, pivot, look up. Who's ahead of the ball?"*

---

## Block 3 — 17 mins
*Walk (5 mins) + Run (12 mins)*

**Concept:** The whole thing — in, ahead, two phases

**Walk it, 5v0 (5 mins)**
- Set the five spots with cones. Walk the screen, the inbound, the pass
  ahead, Phase 1, then Phase 2 with the inbounder trailing
- Do it from both sides: PG starting on the left wing, then the right
- Stop on each pass and ask: *"Who's ahead of the ball now?"*

**Run it against man-to-man pressure (12 mins)**
- 5v3 first: defenders on the PG, S and C. Two attackers are always free —
  the job is to find them
- Then 5v4, then 5v5 if the group is ready
- Scoring: **Phase 1 layup = 3 points. Phase 2 basket = 2. Anything else = 1.**
  The scoring teaches the reads without any extra instruction

**If you have 4:** drop L — C runs to the free-throw line after the pass and
becomes the Phase 1 target
**If you have 6:** the extra player joins the defence, so the break is always
against real pressure

---

## Live Game — 15 mins

**Full court, defence plays man-to-man pressure after every made basket**
- 5v5 if numbers allow, or play the B team if they're there
- Every baseline inbound starts from the shape
- One rule: **at least two passes before the ball crosses halfway**
- Let them play. Stop only to ask *"was Phase 1 on?"* after a missed chance

**End question (sit down, water, one at a time):**
*"Who was ahead of the ball, and did we find her?"*

---

## Coach's Notes

- Did the PG wait for the screen, or go early?
- When the defence switched, did S open back to the ball?
- Did C flash to the ball, or wait to be found?
- Did L read Phase 1 — go when it was on, wait when it wasn't?
- Did the inbounder arrive as the trailer?
- How much did the PG dribble compared with Game 7?
- One thing to carry into the next session — likely: *defensive shape*,
  protecting the driving lanes (the other focus from Game 7)
