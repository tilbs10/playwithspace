# Session 2 — The Sideline, the Pivot and the Outlet

**Theme:** Defence — use the sideline as an extra defender. Offence — pivot into
space, then pass; rebound high and find the outlet box
**Date:** TBC
**Players:** Squad (6 at Game 2; Game 3 needed 2 fill-ins) — set pairs and
groups to whoever turns up
**Duration:** 60 mins
**Equipment:** Half court plus the sideline, a ball per pair, cones (8–10) to
mark lanes and the outlet boxes, a few spots/markers
**Why this session:** Game 3 (W 28-12) showed the girls starting to understand
why we get back — but once the ball crossed halfway they still came out to
meet it, leaving space behind them that the opposition attacked off the
dribble. Some girls are beating the attacker to the key areas, but until they
can control the dribble, getting to the spot still exposes too much space.
The sideline is how we start controlling the dribble. On offence we keep
building the pivot from session 1, and add the first piece of our transition:
the rebound and the outlet.

---

## The Question to Hold All Session

*"Where is the help?"*

On defence, the sideline is help — it never gets tired and it never fouls. On
offence, the space you make with a pivot and the teammate in the outlet box
are help. Keep coming back to that one question.

---

## Warm-up — 8 mins

**Dribble, stop, pivot, find space — everyone with a ball**
- Free dribble in the half court, eyes up
- On **"Stop!"**: jump stop, ball held, then pivot — front pivot first, then
  reverse pivot — and find the patch of floor with the most room
- On **"Go!"**: one dribble into that space, then keep moving
- Last two rounds: do it near the sideline. *"What happens to your room when
  you're next to the line?"* Don't answer it yet — it's Block 1's question
- Breath check at the end: one slow breath, shoulders down

---

## Block 1 — Defence: Use the Sideline — 15 mins
*Instruction (3 mins) + Drill (12 mins)*

**Concept:** Force the dribbler towards the sideline wherever you can, and let
the sideline be your extra defender

**Instruction (3 mins):** Stand a player with the ball in the middle of the
court and ask: *"How many ways can she go?"* Then stand her next to the
sideline and ask again. Let them see it — the line has taken half the floor
away. Name it: **the sideline is a defender that never gets tired and never
fouls.**

Link it straight to Game 3: when we run out to meet the ball, we give her the
middle *and* the space behind us. When we show her the sideline, we take both
away.

**Drill: Sideline channel (1 on 1)**
- Mark a channel with cones a few metres in from the sideline, running up the
  court. Attacker dribbles up the channel, defender starts in front
- **Round 1 — walk:** attacker walks the ball up. Defender stays a step off,
  on the *middle side* of the attacker, hands straight up, and moves her feet
  to keep the attacker heading towards the line
- **Round 2 — live dribble, no reaching:** attacker tries to get to the
  middle; defender's only job is to keep her in the channel and towards the
  sideline. Hands stay up — a reach-in is a foul, not a stop
- **Round 3 — finish the job:** defender wins if the attacker picks up her
  dribble, stops, or steps out. Swap roles every turn
- Coaching cues: *"Show her the sideline." "Middle foot forward." "Don't
  chase — guide."*
- Ask once, mid-drill: *"When did she run out of room? What did you do to
  make that happen?"*

---

## Block 2 — Offence: Pivot, Dribble, Pass — 12 mins
*Instruction (2 mins) + Drill (10 mins)*

**Concept:** Pivot into space, use the dribble to make room, *then* pass

This builds on session 1's escape circle — pivoting and shielding the ball.
Now the pivot leads somewhere: into a dribble that creates space, so the
pass happens from room, not from a crowd.

**Instruction (2 mins):** Show it slowly: catch, pivot away from the
defender, one or two dribbles into open floor, eyes up, pass. Ask: *"Why
dribble before the pass? Why not just pass straight away?"* Let them work out
that the dribble buys a better angle and a calmer pass.

**Drill: Pivot–go–pass (groups of 3: passer, ball-handler, defender)**
- Ball-handler catches with a defender close by
- **Round 1 — passive defender:** pivot away from her, one or two dribbles
  into space, pass to the partner who has found her own bubble
- **Round 2 — light pressure:** defender may move her feet but not reach.
  The ball-handler reads which way to pivot before she goes
- **Round 3:** the defender shows the sideline (Block 1) — the ball-handler
  has to pivot *away from the line* to find her space
- Coaching cue: *"Pivot. Look. Go. Pass."*
- If a pass is wasted, ask: *"Where was the room after your pivot?"*

---

## Block 3 — The Rebound and the Outlet Box — 13 mins
*Instruction (3 mins) + Drill (10 mins)*

**Concept:** Take the rebound above your head, then the first pass goes to
the outlet box

**Instruction (3 mins):** Walk them to the outlet box and mark it with cones
on both sides of the court. *(Mark it where you want it; the usual spot is
wide, near the sideline, around the free-throw line extended, on the side
the rebound comes off.)* Then teach the three things:
- **Where it is** — stand them in it on each side
- **Why we have it** — under the basket is the most crowded place on the
  court. Dribbling out of a crowd loses the ball. One pass to a known spot,
  wide and out of the crowd, gets the ball out fast and starts the break
  before the other team is back
- **Why the ball goes above your head** — on the rebound, hold it high and
  strong where nobody can reach it, not down at your waist where it can be
  knocked away

Ask: *"If everyone knows where the outlet box is, what does the rebounder
not have to do?"* (Look for someone to pass to — she already knows.)

**Drill: Rebound and outlet (groups of 2–3)**
- Coach tosses the ball off the backboard. Rebounder jumps, catches with two
  hands above her head, lands, and keeps it high
- Outlet player sprints to the outlet box on the side the ball came off and
  calls *"Outlet!"*
- Rebounder pivots to the outside and passes to the box. Outlet catches
  facing up the court
- **Round 2:** add a passive defender on the rebounder — now holding it high
  matters
- **Round 3 (if ready):** outlet takes one dribble up the court and passes
  ahead to a third player running the lane — the bridge to fast breaks
- Coaching cues: *"High and strong." "Outside pivot." "Know where she is
  before you land."*

*Likely question:* "If the sideline is a defender, why is the outlet box near
it?" Good — that's real thinking. The outlet *catches and passes on*; she
isn't dribbling into the corner. Let them reason it out.

---

## Live Game — 10 mins

**3v3 (or what numbers allow), half court with a clear-out**
- Two rules, one for each end:
  - **On defence:** show the dribbler the sideline
  - **After a defensive rebound:** the first pass goes to the outlet box
    before you attack
- Bonus point for a stop made against the sideline, and for a clean
  rebound-to-outlet
- Let them play. Stop only for a "freeze" when someone runs out to meet the
  ball — *"What's behind you right now?"*

**End question (sit down, water, one at a time):**
*"When did the sideline help you today?"* — then — *"Who can tell me why we
have an outlet box?"* (They should explain the why, not just point to it.)

---

## Coach's Notes

- Who still runs out to meet the ball — and does showing the sideline change it?
- Who keeps her hands straight up under pressure, and who starts reaching?
- Who holds the rebound high, and who brings it down to her waist?
- Who goes to the outlet box without being told?
- On offence, who pivots away from pressure naturally, and who turns into it?
- Nerves: being trapped against the sideline can feel like panic for the
  attacker. Notice who tightens up — and normalise it
- One thing to carry into session 3 — likely: *the outlet's next pass —
  running the lanes in transition*
