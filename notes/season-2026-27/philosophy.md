## The Central Question

> Can you teach kids to *see* the game the way good players see it?

Unchanged. Not plays, not memorised positions — court vision: reading space,
reading the defence, deciding under pressure.

---

## The One Thing

**Where to go.** Not just where to stand, but why.

- **Spacing** — where to be and why those spots matter
- **Transition** — running the lanes, filling space before the defence sets
- **Team defence** — defending as a unit, not five individuals
- **Defensive adjustment** — the first rotation, what to do when the first
  defender is beaten

---

## How I Coach

**Calm and questioning.** Curiosity before correction: *What did you see?
Where did you think the space was? What would you do differently?*

**Nervous systems.** Notice when you're activated, come back to ease. Breath,
body awareness, no catastrophising a turnover. The game should feel like play.

---

## Where We're Starting

**Game one came before session one.** No training, no shared language, no
instruction — so what the girls did that day is a clean baseline: what they
do with space *by default*. That's more useful than any assessment I could
design.

Questions to answer from it (see `game-01-capture.md`):
- Where did they stand when they didn't have the ball?
- Did the ball move, or did it get held?
- What did they do when it went wrong — recover, or spiral?

The first training session is built from those answers, not from the
generic plan.

---

## What I'm Not Trying to Do

- Win at all costs (winning is a by-product)
- Overload players with information — one concept at a time, embodied
  before moving on
- Coach through fear or correction alone
- Produce technically perfect players who don't understand *why*

---

## Mantra

*See it. Feel it. Play it.*
